Morning in the small town began with the smell of fresh bread. The baker opened the shutters, put a basket of buns on the windowsill and watched the first passers-by walk slowly down the street. The baker's cat sat on the porch and squinted at the sun. He knew that soon someone would bring him a saucer of milk, and so he was in no hurry to go anywhere.

On the corner of the street stood an old library. Its doors opened at exactly nine o'clock, and the librarian always arrived ten minutes early. He watered the flowers, wiped the dust from the long shelves and laid out the new magazines on the table. Children loved to come here after school: it was quiet and warm, and they could read books about travels, stars and distant seas.

One day a girl with a thick notebook came to the library. She said that she wanted to learn to understand how machines think. The librarian smiled and brought her three books: about numbers, about logic and about languages. The girl opened the first book and read that any number can be written with zeros and ones. This seemed amazing to her, and she began copying the examples into her notebook.

In the evening she told her brother about it. Her brother was older and studied at the university. He explained that a computer stores not only numbers but also letters, pictures and music, and all of it turns into long sequences of zeros and ones. Then he added that modern programs can predict the next word in a sentence. If a program has read a great many texts, it starts guessing words so well that it seems as if it understands the meaning.

The girl thought about it. It seemed to her that guessing the next word was easy. Her brother suggested a game: he would say the beginning of a phrase, and she would continue it. He said: the cat is sitting on. The girl answered: on the roof. He said: in the morning the baker bakes. The girl answered: bread. Her brother laughed and said that she had just done the same thing a language model does. It chooses the most probable continuation, based on what it has seen before.
